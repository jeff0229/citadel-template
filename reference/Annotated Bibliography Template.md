# Annotated Bibliography Template

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from a UK university library workshop on annotated bibliographies. Topic-neutral procedural template for any annotated-bibliography entry, whether a standalone assignment or a dissertation appendix. Whether your dissertation requires one is a question for your supervisor or programme.*

## What it informs

An annotated-bibliography appendix or assignment, if required. Source-selection logic for the literature review (transferable).

## Structural rules and framing moves per entry

- **Three components in fixed order.** (1) Full citation in the required style, (2) summary, (3) annotation/evaluation.
- **Total length** roughly 150 words per entry.
- **Summary** about 50 to 70 words (less than half the paragraph). Covers the main argument or thesis, the evidence, the methodology, the findings, and any future directions the author suggests.
- **Annotation/evaluation** about 100 words (around half the paragraph). Covers the author's credentials, the quality of the references used, the accessibility of the writing, the soundness of the reasoning, the sufficiency of evidence versus opinion, and a final one-or-two-sentence relevance verdict for your project.
- **Use full sentences and your own words** (no copy-paste). Objective register. Ban vague phrases ("pretty good").
- **Selection criteria for what to annotate.** Established authorship, reputable publisher, source variety (articles plus books plus theses plus foreign-language where applicable), and currency (3 to 5 years recent, or a classic).
- **Standard order** is Citation, Summary, Evaluation. A Citation, Evaluation, Summary variation exists but is an edge case. Default to the standard order.

## Exemplar passages worth modelling

> "Make less than half of the paragraph or 50 to 70 words / Clearly and concisely / Write full sentences / Use your own words... What is the main argument, thesis or claim? What evidence is presented to support that argument, thesis or claim? What methodology is used? What are most important findings or conclusions? Does the author suggest any future directions for research?"

The five-question summary skeleton for each entry.

> "Final general comment on how relevant and valuable this article or book is for your research topic, and for someone else (in one or two sentences)"

The closing-sentence move: every annotation ends with a project-specific relevance verdict, not a generic evaluation. Use this as the final beat in every entry.

## Caveats

- The source is a workshop slide, not a worked exemplar. Treat the rules as load-bearing, and look at past dissertation appendices or library examples for actual prose models.
- The 150-word target is a guideline, not a hard limit. Any assignment brief overrides it.
- The "author credentials / reputable publisher" framing tilts toward source-vetting. For a topic that spans languages, regions, or disciplines, also weigh positionality (geographic, linguistic, disciplinary) rather than credentials alone.
