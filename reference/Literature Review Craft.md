# Literature Review Craft

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from a UK university study guide on writing an effective literature review. Fully topic-neutral and directly templatable for any narrative literature review.*

## What it informs

The Literature Review chapter primarily. The Introduction (where embedded literature engagement happens). Any chapter that recruits sources to support a claim.

## Structural rules and framing moves

- **Three-part LR structure.** Introduction (scope, your position, what is excluded) then main body (synthesis) then conclusion (tie-up, not recap).
- **Every paragraph opens with a topic sentence** stating what the paragraph will do.
- **Two moves are required to demonstrate critical engagement:**
  1. Integration. Weave threads via similarities and differences across sources, using signpost connectors ("Similarly", "In contrast", "concurred with", and the like).
  2. Analytical step-back. Ask "so what does that all mean?", "why are there conflicting findings?", "what is missing?".
- **Replace the laundry list with synthesis.** Each source is recruited to do work, not summarised serially.
- **The Introduction states scope AND what is not covered.** No source citations are needed in the LR introduction itself.
- **The Conclusion adjudicates, identifies gaps, or segues into your own research.** It must not just recap.
- **Narrative review** (judgement-based source selection) is the default for most qualitative dissertations, not systematic review. State your selection logic either way.
- **Over-reference in early drafts, trim later.** Better to over-cite and prune than to under-cite and miss a landmark study.
- **Never use exclamation marks.**
- **Examiners' top complaints** (load-bearing): failure to use recent literature, parochialism, excluding landmark studies, and no critical assessment.

## Exemplar passages worth modelling

> "Human behaviour is complex, and many theories have been proposed to explain what motivates it. Although the literature covers a wide variety of such theories, this review will deal with three major themes that emerge repeatedly in published works. These themes are: the influence of rewards; the influence of peer pressure; and the influence of moral frameworks in which we operate. Although the literature presents these themes in different contexts, this review will primarily focus on their application to how we behave in times of crisis."

The canonical four-sentence LR-introduction pattern: topic statement, scope refinement, named themes, narrowed focus. Use the structure to open your own LR with your own two or three themes. Do not lift the wording.

> "Schmidt and Cohen (2009) reported... However, Dexter and Yamamoto (2012)... found no such increase. An explanation for these apparently contradictory findings may be found when their respective study designs are examined. Tufts and Larson (2002) have previously demonstrated... A novel approach that avoids this potential source of error was recently presented by McDonald and Murphy (2021)..."

The integration-plus-step-back move: sources contrasted, a methodological explanation offered for the contradiction, a third source recruited to adjudicate. (Author names here are generic stand-ins.) Use this shape whenever you engage a contested claim in your field.

## Caveats

- The exemplar names are generic stand-ins. Borrow only the structure, never the wording.
- The original guide writes from a biomedical-leaning frame. The "controversy then adjudicate" move maps cleanly onto qualitative and interpretive debates, but do not force a quantitative-style adjudication where interpretive synthesis is the actual move.
- Pairs with [[Systematic vs Narrative Review]] (a contrast reference). Borrow that document's transparency norms (document inclusion criteria, search terms) without importing the systematic-review machinery.
