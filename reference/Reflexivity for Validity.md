# Reflexivity for Validity

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from Holt (2013), "Reflexivity as a way to ensure validity in a small qualitative research project" (Education sector, small interview pilot). Borrow the conceptual moves, not the empirical content. Pair with [[Reflexive Journal Writing]] for prose templates.*

## What it informs

The Methodology, the Reflexivity section, the Limitations, and the reporting-and-transparency rationale.

## Structural rules and framing moves

- **Open by rejecting positivist validity transplants.** Standard positivist validity tests are not an appropriate or plausible way to demonstrate validity in qualitative research. Nominate reflexivity as the chosen route instead.
- **Frame validation as socially constructed and trust-based** (Mishler 1990). The reader, not a metric, evaluates validity. Transparency is therefore the duty.
- **Use Pillow's (2003) four reflexivity strategies as a structuring device.** Recognition of self, recognition of other, truth, and transcendence.
- **Use Reinharz's (1997) three selves as a vocabulary for positionality moves.** The research-based self, the brought self, and the self created in the field.
- **Walk through reflexivity stage by stage.** Planning, sample, interviewing, analysis, reporting. Each stage gets its own reflexive accounting.
- **Address sample validity head-on.** Disclaim generalisability. Reframe the sample as yielding "rich and unique data" rather than representative data.
- **Treat member-checking as both ethical and validity-bearing.** Report what changed when participants pushed back.
- **Acknowledge the limits of reflexivity itself.** Self-awareness alone does not guarantee reliability; full transparency is more an ideal than achievable in practice.
- **Close with an admission of honest uncertainty** without retreating from the reflexive stance. Model honest hedging.

## Exemplar passages worth modelling

> "I maintained a focus on self throughout, who I am, how I feel, what my responses are, my emerging relationship to the participants and the impact of this on findings and analysis."

A compact statement of what reflexive practice operationally entails. A useful template for the Methodology chapter's reflexivity declaration.

> "It is not enough merely to acknowledge bias when writing up; the receiver of the research needs to see how awareness of bias influenced the whole process, ideally before they are given the findings."

Justifies foregrounding reflexivity rather than relegating it to an appendix. Directly applicable to chapter-ordering decisions.

> "I was not seeking 'external validity' or generalisability, so how was the sample relevant?... I would not be claiming that the sample was representative... but that the sample provided opportunities for rich and unique data."

A template for handling the sample-size or representativeness challenge in a small interview study.

## Caveats

- The source is a small Education-sector pilot. Borrow the moves, not the substance; your empirical world will differ.
- The author is herself a novice researcher writing reflectively. Her hedging register suits a Master's dissertation, but calibrate it against your programme's expected register.
- The source conflates "validity" and "trustworthiness" loosely. If you cite the framework, pair it with a standard qualitative-validity source (for example Braun and Clarke, or Lincoln and Guba) rather than relying on the pilot alone.
