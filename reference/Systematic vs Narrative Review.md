# Systematic vs Narrative Review (Contrast Reference)

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from a UK university lecture on systematic literature reviews. If your method is a narrative or qualitative review rather than a systematic one, read this to understand what you are NOT doing, and to borrow the transparency norms without importing the formal machinery.*

## What it informs

The Methodology (search-strategy transparency only). The Literature Review chapter (selectively, for source-selection logic).

## Moves a narrative review SHOULD adopt

- **Document inclusion and exclusion criteria explicitly**, even for a narrative review (what counted as relevant, what was set aside).
- **Record databases, date ranges, search terms, and language restrictions** for any systematic component (for example, if you searched a literature systematically for one strand of the review).
- **ISAS skeleton** (Identify, Select, Appraise, Synthesise) as a useful internal checklist even when not formalised.
- **Distinguish "literature" from "not literature" early.** Peer-reviewed articles, monographs, and theses are in. Wikipedia, blogs, and encyclopedias are out as scholarship (acknowledge them separately if you use grey or digital sources as primary data rather than as scholarship).
- **Note publication and language bias** as a caveat on coverage, especially where your topic spans languages or regions and much of the scholarship is in one language.

## Moves to SKIP for a qualitative or narrative review

- PICOS / SPIDER frameworks (designed for intervention studies).
- PRISMA flow diagrams.
- Meta-analysis, effect size, heterogeneity pooling.
- Quality-appraisal checklists (such as COREQ, Caldwell) used as gatekeepers for inclusion.
- A concept-matrix tabulation of all included studies as the synthesis backbone.

## Exemplar passage worth modelling

> "a systematic, transparent means for gathering, synthesising and appraising findings of studies on a particular topic or question." (Sweet and Moynihan 2007)

The borrowable word is "transparent". Even narrative reviews owe the reader transparency about how sources were chosen. Use this to frame your LR's source-selection paragraph.

## Caveats

- This is a contrast object. Do not import the formal apparatus into a narrative review.
- If your LR needs to defend a non-systematic approach explicitly, Hammersley (2001) is referenced in the source as a "narrative response" to systematic review and is a useful citation for that argument. Worth tracking down if you make that case.
