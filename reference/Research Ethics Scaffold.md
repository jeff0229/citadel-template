# Research Ethics Scaffold

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from a UK postgraduate research-ethics guidance deck. The conceptual scaffold (the seven issues, the design/in-practice split, the confidentiality/anonymity distinction, contextual vulnerability) is portable across topics. Risk-level labels, named committees, and discipline-specific guideline bodies are institution- and discipline-specific. Verify those against your own programme.*

## What it informs

The ethics section or chapter primarily. The Methodology (where consent and recruitment design live). The Discussion (limits of data, reflexivity).

## Structural rules and framing moves

- **State your risk level explicitly and justify it.** Low-risk, supervisor-approved projects (non-clinical, non-vulnerable, non-deceptive, adult participants, public material) should say so and explain why. Use your own institution's risk-level labels.
- **Ethics is a dynamic process, not a one-off form.** Distinguish ethical research (what you strive towards) from the ethics form (a basic institutional checkpoint). The ethics section should narrate ongoing in-practice ethics, not just consent-form mechanics.
- **Seven-issue scaffold for the ethics section.** Address each, even briefly:
  1. Autonomy and informed consent
  2. Confidentiality and anonymity
  3. Power and vulnerability
  4. Incentives
  5. Conflict of interest
  6. Harms to the researcher
  7. Data management
- **Confidentiality versus anonymity.** Confidentiality means modifying identifiable information. Anonymity means collecting without identifiable data. Quoting public posts verbatim can be de-anonymising even when no name is attached (the text is searchable back to its author). Name this complication explicitly.
- **Vulnerability is contextual, not categorical.** Avoid blanket definitions. Ask: how, for whom, and in what way might this research make a participant vulnerable? Participants who are not "vulnerable" in the institutional sense may become situationally or relationally vulnerable. Treat vulnerability as situational and relational, and design mitigations for it.
- **Reflexivity is mandated under power.** The Methodology must include a reflexive paragraph on the researcher's positionality. (See [[Reflexivity for Validity]].)
- **Data management.** Specify storage location, retention period, and deletion plan. Many institutions bar students from retaining data for future student re-use, so do not promise that in a consent form. Check your own rule.
- **First-person responsibility.** Responsibility for the conduct of research rests with you as the principal investigator. Frame the ethics section in first-person responsibility, not as something delegated to the institution.
- **Cite your discipline's professional ethics guidelines** where applicable. Naming the relevant disciplinary body's research-ethics guidance signals competence. Identify the body for your own field.
- **Online or social-media research carries named complications**, especially around consent for material that is public but not posted with research in mind. Name the complication rather than glossing over it.

## Two-part architecture for the ethics section

> "Conducting research ethically entails 2 main components: 1. How to design your study 2. Delivering the study protocol in an ethical way: 'in-practice ethics' (Guillemin and Gillam, 2004)"

A clean two-part architecture: design ethics (what was built into the study) and in-practice ethics (the judgement calls made during fieldwork). Guillemin and Gillam (2004) is a real, citeable source.

> "Differences between ethical research (which we should all strive towards) & ethics forms (a basic institutional check-point)"

Registers that institutional approval is a floor, not a ceiling. Signals sophistication without sounding sanctimonious.

> "Vulnerabilities can be: Situational; Relational. Fluctuate in the course of research due to changing context or individual circumstances"

Template for treating participants' vulnerability as situational and relational rather than categorical, and for justifying why your participants are not "vulnerable" in the institutional sense while still designing for the contextual vulnerability they may experience.

## Caveats

- Source decks are often slide extractions with formatting artefacts. Pull verbatim only from clean prose blocks; treat mangled headings as gist.
- Clinical, custodial, mental-capacity, and child-protection regimes were not relevant to a low-risk social-research project and are skipped. Confirm none apply to yours.
- The detailed dissertation ethics-review process is specified by your own dissertation handbook. Cross-check.
