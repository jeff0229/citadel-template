# Submission Compliance and Data Protection

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from a UK school-wide postgraduate-taught student handbook. This is a compliance reference, not a voice reference: use it for the procedural sequence and the data-protection framing, not for prose. Every concrete figure below (deadline, thresholds, section numbers, fine amount) is institution- and year-specific. Verify each against your own programme handbook.*

## What it informs

The ethics section primarily. Data-protection framing in the Methodology. Whole-document procedural compliance (submission, late penalties, word-count penalties).

## Structural rules and framing moves

- **Submission deadline is a hard deadline.** Lateness penalties apply automatically. Find your own date and treat it as load-bearing. (The source handbook named a specific August date for its cohort; yours will differ. Verify.)
- **Pass thresholds.** Typical UK postgraduate-taught bands are Pass at or above 50%, Merit around 60%, Distinction around 70%, with borderline rules that reward consistency between coursework and dissertation marks. Confirm your own programme's exact bands.
- **Word-count penalty is usually automatic** (no academic judgement applied). Stay under whatever your dissertation handbook specifies.
- **Ethics precondition sequence.** Before using personal data, the standard institutional sequence is: become familiar with the data-protection guidance, discuss implications with your supervisor, seek ethics approval, and obtain consent from data subjects. Mirror this sequence in your ethics section.
- **Data-protection breach is an institutional offence**, framed in terms of large regulatory fines and reputational damage. Cite this when justifying conservative anonymisation choices for participants.
- **Live transcription or translation services that upload to third parties may be prohibited.** Relevant if interviewing in a language you do not transcribe yourself. Prefer offline tools or document-grade transcription. Check your own institution's rule.
- **Digital safety and citizenship.** Framed as a social responsibility to treat others with dignity and respect. Useful framing if your ethics section discusses observation of public posts.

## Exemplar passage worth modelling

> "Before using personal data as part of their studies students must become familiar with the linked guidance, discuss implications with their supervisor and seek appropriate ethics approval. They must also obtain consent from the data subjects to take part in the studies."

Gives the exact procedural sequence: supervisor consultation, then ethics approval, then consent. Use it as the spine for the ethics-process paragraph.

## Caveats

- This is a procedural document. Almost no prose is worth templating from it. Use it for compliance facts, not voice.
- Dissertation-specific structural and word-count rules are NOT here. They live in your dissertation handbook (see [[Dissertation Structure and Argument]]).
- Much of a student handbook (general assessment regulations, mitigations, conduct) is not dissertation-specific. Skip those sections when drafting.
