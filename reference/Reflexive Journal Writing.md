# Reflexive Journal Writing (Prose Templates)

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from Holt (2019), extracts from the reflexive journal of a real qualitative dissertation. Used as prose templates for reflexive writing. The exemplar quotes are the source author's own (a study of mental-health promotion in schools); borrow the affective texture and the move sequence, not the substantive content.*

## What it informs

The Reflexivity section, the Methodology (interview and analysis subsections), and appendix journal samples.

## Structural rules and framing moves

- **Title each entry by reflexive function, not by date.** For example: "Reflexivity, the impact of seeking negative cases", "Reflexivity and ethical considerations", "Reflexivity, researcher in relationship with participants", "Reflexivity and analysis". This implies thematic, not chronological, integration into the report.
- **Voice is first-person, past tense, confessional but analytical.** Admit the emotion ("I was alarmed", "almost despairing", "annoyed with myself"), then immediately theorise it via a cited concept (for example Reinharz's selves or Pillow's strategies).
- **Standard move sequence per entry.** (1) Describe a moment. (2) Name the emotion or bias. (3) Hook to a theoretical category (such as the "brought self" or the "self created in the field"). (4) State what changed in conduct or interpretation. (5) Name a residual limit.
- **Entries focus on micro-incidents** (one interview, one phrase, one re-listening pass) rather than broad reflections. Templatable as per-interview entries.
- **Use a measured reprimand-of-self** ("I silently reprimanded myself", "I revisited the transcript, annoyed with myself"). This gives entries narrative texture without melodrama.
- **Flag what could not be recovered** (body language not recorded, ephemeral impressions lost). A model for limitations writing.
- **Integration is inline.** Extracts read as inserts within a Findings or Methodology narrative, not as a standalone diary. The report quotes them and theorises around them.

## Exemplar passages worth modelling

> "I was alarmed to find myself feeling dismayed when she spoke. I realised that I was pushing her for negative views with my repeated questions about barriers to promotion. I was glossing over all the positives... Fortunately, I became aware of this particular 'creation' of myself' (Reinharz, 1997) in the interview process in time to be able to respond more appropriately."

A gold-standard entry: names the bias, catches it mid-interview, theorises it, reports the corrective. A direct template for an entry on your own positionality, for example catching yourself steering an interviewee toward a thesis you arrived with.

> "Participant K spoke of a pupil who had died, and suddenly seemed overcome with a strong emotion. She paused, looking at me with tears in her eyes. Tears came to my eyes. There was no need to speak... Platitudes would have been inappropriate, as would a carefully composed verbal response."

Models how to write an emotionally heavy moment without sentimentality: short sentences, no over-explanation. A template for sensitive interview moments in any topic.

> "It was only on listening back to the recordings that I realised that I had glossed over some of the areas that I might have probed further with another participant... It is possible to be committed, enthusiastic but ill informed... I could have taken less at face value but this may have damaged the emerging relationship and inhibited the conversation."

Models honest reporting of a methodological compromise: acknowledges what was traded off and why, without retreating into apology. Useful for limitations and for analysis-stage reflexivity.

## Caveats

- Cadence is not fixed. Entries appear written per-interview or per-listening-pass, not daily. Treat them as event-triggered rather than diaristic.
- Typical entry length is roughly 250 to 400 words: short enough to embed inline in a report, not appendix-only.
- Use these prose templates alongside the conceptual framework in [[Reflexivity for Validity]] (same author). The framework supplies the vocabulary; these extracts supply the prose.
- The source topic differs from yours. The affective texture transfers; the substantive content does not.
