# Programme Remit and Positioning

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from a taught-MSc programme handbook. This is the thinnest, most programme-specific reference in the pack: it tells you to position your work inside your own programme's stated remit, so its concrete substance must come from your own programme handbook and Learn pages. Verify all specifics there.*

## What it informs

All chapters, indirectly. Most directly the Introduction (where you position the work) and the Methodology (where you justify your methodological choice against the alternatives your programme offers).

## Structural rules and framing moves

- A taught-MSc dissertation is **self-designed independent research with supervisory support**. Frame it as a contribution to your programme's discipline, not as a free-floating project.
- Read your programme's **stated aims and intended learning outcomes**, then position the dissertation as hitting them explicitly. Most programmes name some mix of: critical analysis within the field, methodological reflection, and ethical engagement. A strong dissertation visibly satisfies all of its programme's named outcomes.
- **Interdisciplinary borrowing is usually sanctioned, not a deviation**, where the programme says so. If your programme outcome names interdisciplinary competence, pulling from adjacent fields is licensed.
- **Justify your methodological approach explicitly.** Where a programme offers more than one pathway (for example a qualitative track and a computational or data-science track), the Methodology must defend the approach you chose against the one you did not.
- **Treat ethics as a programme outcome, not just a hurdle.** Where relevant, foreground the issues specific to your method (for online research: the public/private distinction, platform Terms of Service, the vulnerability of online speech) alongside generic consent and anonymity.
- Note any tolerance the programme signals for **non-traditional registers** (blog posts, other media) as evidence of remit, but keep the dissertation prose itself formal.

## Exemplar framing moves

> Position the dissertation as forcing reconsideration of an existing question in your field, rather than as a niche study of a single case or platform.

Frame the Introduction's stake-claiming sentence around the broader question your work re-opens, then narrow to your case.

> Echo your programme's own stated aim near-verbatim when you state the dissertation's contribution.

Aligning your contribution claim with the programme's published aim signals fit to the examiner.

## Caveats

- **Word count is usually NOT specified at this level.** Programme handbooks often defer to the school-wide dissertation handbook and to Learn pages. Confirm your figure against both, and against your supervisor.
- Programme handbooks rarely specify dissertation structure beyond gesturing at the school-wide handbook. Treat the school-wide handbook's structural rules as canonical (see [[Dissertation Structure and Argument]]).
- Programme name and any in-document typos: use the official programme title consistently in dissertation-facing prose.
