# Methodology and Trustworthiness

*Reusable craft reference. Derived from a UK university methodology-orientation lecture (Arshad). Light-touch but useful for the trustworthiness vocabulary it introduces and for its "be realistic" framing. Topic-neutral.*

## What it informs

Methodology chapter framing. The Introduction (scoping). Light cross-cutting on adaptability and supervisor consultation.

## Structural rules and framing moves

- **Realism in scoping.** Plans do not always work out. The ability to adapt is key. Have a Plan B.
- **Methodology versus method.** Methodology is the rationale; method is the tool. The methodology decides which methods you choose.
- **Three credibility constructs in qualitative research.**
  1. Validity. The extent to which a concept can be measured accurately (content validity, criterion validity).
  2. Reliability. Following Kirk and Miller (1986, pp. 41 to 42), three types: stability of measurement when given repeatedly, stability over time, and similarity within a given period.
  3. Trustworthiness. The qualitative-appropriate substitute for validity and reliability: trustworthiness, credibility, dependability. Triangulation supports dependability.
- **The key methodological test is "defensible".** The methodology, method, and analysis together must establish confidence and be trustworthy.
- **Relevance.** Research should address real concerns and, where possible, lead to new knowledge or change. Translate findings for non-academic audiences. State the relevance at the end of the dissertation.
- **A method is a guide, not an A-to-Z roadmap.** Take a method as a guide rather than worrying about following it exactly. Speak to your supervisor for advice.

## Exemplar passages worth modelling

> "MORE APPROPRIATE TERMS FOR QUALITATIVE RESEARCH WOULD BE TRUSTWORTHINESS, CREDIBILITY, DEPENDABILITY. QUALITATIVE RESEARCH AS IT INVOLVES PEOPLE'S LIVES WILL HAVE DATA THAT MIGHT CHANGE DEPENDING ON WHEN YOU GATHERED THE DATA."

Supplies the vocabulary for the Methodology chapter's quality-criteria paragraph. Use trustworthiness, credibility, dependability, and triangulation rather than validity and reliability. Pairs with Lincoln and Guba's standard four-criterion qualitative framework.

> "TAKE IT AS A GUIDE RATHER THAN GETTING WORRIED YOU ARE NOT FOLLOWING IT LIKE AN A-Z GUIDE."

Licence to depart from method-textbook prescriptions when the field requires it. Useful when explaining adaptations to interview structure, recruitment, or analysis sequencing.

## Caveats

- The lecture is short and education-research flavoured; the worked example does not transfer to other topics, but the methodology vocabulary does.
- The Kirk and Miller (1986) reliability triad is worth tracking down if you want reliability framing in the Methodology chapter. Full citation: Kirk, J., and Miller, M. L. (1986). Reliability and validity in qualitative research. Beverly Hills: Sage Publications.
- The lecture predates the ethical complexity of some online or covert-observation designs and does not address those. Look elsewhere for adversarial observation, account-creation positionality, or re-identification risk.
